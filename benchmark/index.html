    The public MovieLens benchmark

    An external, reproducible quality check that does not depend on this repo's own tuning: MovieLens (ml-latest-small) ratings converted into preference judgments, scored by this engine and four simple baselines on the same catalog.

    What the judgments are

    eval/ml_judgments.jsonl — 150 seeds, 10 good + 10 bad each (2.25M good×bad pairs), derived offline and deterministically by eval/build_ml_bench.py judgments:

    • a seed is a movie at least 50 MovieLens users rated ≥ 4.0;
    • good = the ten other movies most agreed-upon (≥ 5 agreements, ties by id) among users who rated the seed ≥ 4.0;
    • bad = the ten movies that same loving cohort most agreed it rated ≤ 2.5.

    This is collaborative ground truth — what people who loved the seed also loved vs. disliked — independent of any content features, which is exactly what a content-based engine should be measured against.

    Reproducing

    # 1. judgments (offline, no key) — already committed, this regenerates:
python eval/build_ml_bench.py judgments --ml ml-latest-small.zip \
    --seed-lovers-min 50 --max-seeds 150 --out eval/ml_judgments.jsonl

# 2. catalog — bootstraps a DB with the TMDB mirror for the benchmark
#    universe (~every linked movie with ≥5 ratings; one API call per
#    title, a couple of minutes) + MiniLM embeddings:
pip install -e ".[pg,bench]"
python eval/build_ml_bench.py catalog --ml ml-latest-small.zip \
    --dsn postgresql://demo:demo@localhost:5432/mlbench \
    --api-key $TMDB_API_KEY --embed

# 3. score — five methods, one table:
python eval/eval.py --dsn postgresql://demo:demo@localhost:5432/mlbench \
    --judgments eval/ml_judgments.jsonl

    Methods: engine, tmdb_sim (TMDB's own recommendations graph), minilm_cosine (overview embeddings), popularity (vote_count), keywords (keyword-set Jaccard). The scoring run needs CINE_REC_EMBEDDING=minilm so the engine reads the benchmark's embedding column.

    Results (2026-10-21, catalog of 3,636 titles)

    method pairwise acc. NDCG@10 coverage@10 novelty@10
    tmdb_sim 0.02 0.06 0.18 11.4
    minilm_cosine 0.60 0.03 0.28 12.1
    popularity 0.96 0.16 0.00 8.5
    keywords 0.33 0.03 0.27 11.8
    engine 0.45 0.05 0.26 12.1

    How to read this — the ground truth is mass agreement: movies the seed's loving cohort also loved, vs. movies that cohort disliked.

    • Popularity wins by construction. Cohort-agreement picks famous titles (every good/bad id here passes a 500-vote floor), so ranking by vote_count is nearly the answer key. Its coverage@10 of 0.00 is the tell: the same ten titles for every seed — precisely the block-repeat failure mode list diversity exists to avoid.
    • TMDB's own graph scores 0.02 — below random: famous-but-disliked titles sit in its "people also liked" head, so it actively prefers the bad side.
    • The engine measures a different thing. It optimizes taste similarity (auteur signals, keywords, plot structure) with diversity reranking — its home ground is the taste-judgment eval in the README, where the single-signal baselines win their own construction instead. Pure plot-cosine (0.60) aligns with mass agreement better than the engine's taste mix; that is a real property of content-based taste ranking, reported as-is.
    • NDCG@10 discriminates weakly here (10 goods in a 3,636-title pool — even an oracle caps low); pairwise and coverage carry the signal.
